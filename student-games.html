A vehicular wave-defense game where the player rams, tricks, and power-ups their way through escalating enemy hordes to protect and repair a central objective. Power-ups are tuned to scale exponentially, meaning specialization beats generalism: a deliberate risk/reward design.

Design decision: Playtesting revealed a core feel problem: enemies clustering on the objective meant the player kept accidentally hitting it when trying to ram enemies, killing momentum and punishing aggression. My solution was a one-way bubble shield. Enemies push against it from outside, but the player drives through freely. This separated the collision domains cleanly and made aggressive play feel satisfying rather than frustrating.

What I did

  • Designed the exponential power-up system
  • Tuned enemy waves on a sine-wave difficulty curve, alternating pressure with breathing room
  • Designed the one-way bubble shield around the objective

A first-person sci-fi puzzle-action game built around physics-based powers: telekinesis, black hole projection, and gravity inversion.

Design decision: I designed gravity inversion as a puzzle tool rather than a combat ability. Each level teaches the player a new spatial logic, so the level layouts were built around what the player can flip and move, not what they can shoot.

What I did

  • Owned the design of all core systems, mechanics and level layouts
  • Programmed the player's three powers: telekinesis, black hole and gravity inversion

A mobile arcade game with themed worlds that mixes vertical platforming and endless running across 10 to 12 levels.

Design decision: Every character has a unique ability in every game mode, laid out as a 2D character x mode matrix. Each combination plays differently, while each new character or mode only adds one row or column of abilities to design.

What I did

  • Built every level in the game
  • Balanced how each power-up plays across the levels and modes
  • Wrote the game's narrative design
