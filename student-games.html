Bumping Mayhem

Type
School project
School
Howest DAE
Role
Game Lead & Lead Designer
Team
3
Engine
Unity
Duration
2 months

A vehicular wave-defense game where the player rams, tricks, and power-ups their way through escalating enemy hordes to protect and repair a central objective. Power-ups are tuned to scale exponentially, meaning specialization beats generalism: a deliberate risk/reward design.

Design decision: Playtesting revealed a core feel problem: enemies clustering on the objective meant the player kept accidentally hitting it when trying to ram enemies, killing momentum and punishing aggression. My solution was a one-way bubble shield. Enemies push against it from outside, but the player drives through freely. This separated the collision domains cleanly and made aggressive play feel satisfying rather than frustrating.

What I did

  • Designed the exponential power-up system
  • Tuned enemy waves on a sine-wave difficulty curve, alternating pressure with breathing room

Warped

Type
School project
School
The Open University
Role
Game Lead & Lead Designer
Team
6
Engine
Unity
Duration
3 months

A first-person sci-fi puzzle-action game built around physics-based powers: telekinesis, black hole projection, and gravity inversion.

Design decision: The gravity inversion power was my concept, designed specifically as a puzzle-solving tool rather than a combat ability. Each level was built around teaching the player a new spatial logic: what happens to enemies, objects, and the player themselves when “down” becomes “up.” That single design decision shaped the entire level design language of the game.

What I did

  • Owned the design of all core systems, mechanics and level layouts
  • Programmed the player's three powers: telekinesis, black hole and gravity inversion

Worldly Adventures

Type
School project
School
The Open University
Role
Game Designer & Level Designer
Team
5
Engine
Unity
Duration
3 months

A mobile arcade game with themed worlds (jungle, toy world, and more), combining vertical platformer and endless runner mechanics across 10-12 levels.

Design decision: I designed the character power system as a 2D matrix: each character has a unique ability per game mode, so the sloth's runner power and platformer power are completely different, as are every other character's. This meant every character-mode combination felt distinct without requiring separate content pipelines, giving the game replayability while keeping production manageable for a student team.

What I did

  • Built every level in the game
  • Balanced how each power-up plays across the levels and modes
  • Wrote the game's narrative design
